# ZC-HR-REVIEW-001: Independent peer-manager review

**Status:** Proposed fictional policy

## Right to request
An employee disputing a management decision can request review by a qualified manager **outside the original reporting chain**. The request cannot be blocked by the disputed manager.

## Selection and independence
- Select from a documented pool using rotation or auditable assignment; avoid hand-picking by the accused manager.
- Reviewer discloses reporting relationships, personal ties, prior involvement and other conflicts.
- Employee and manager may raise a reasoned conflict objection; independent administrator decides recusal.
- For small organizations without an independent manager, use an external neutral reviewer or disclose the limitation rather than pretending independence.

## Procedure
1. Register the dispute and preserve relevant evidence.
2. Notify affected parties of scope, confidentiality limits, and process.
3. Allow both sides to provide documents and a response; accommodate communication needs.
4. Separate fact findings from recommendations; apply published standards.
5. Issue a reasoned written decision with evidence references, redactions, and a correction process.
6. Provide appeal to a different reviewer or independent panel; protect against retaliation.
7. Audit reviewer assignments and outcomes for conflicts, delays and disparate treatment.

## Authority boundaries
Peer review is not a substitute for mandatory legal reporting, a formal discrimination investigation, collective bargaining rights, or urgent safety intervention. The authority to pause, reverse, or recommend decisions must be explicitly established by future governance decisions.

## Fiction stress tests
- All managers know one another.
- The reviewer previously supported the original decision.
- An employee challenges a scheduling change with incomplete records.
- Reviewer finds the employee wrong but the manager's process unfair.
- Manager retaliates after an appeal.
