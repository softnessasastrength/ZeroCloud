# CASE-LD-001: Three teams, one incident

**Status:** Fictional stress-test outline. No outcome selected.

## Common facts
A scheduled software release is approaching. A junior engineer identifies a plausible authorization flaw. The team's service owner wants to ship; a security reviewer requests a delay. A manager changes the engineer's on-call schedule shortly after the disagreement. There is insufficient evidence initially to determine whether the schedule change was retaliatory.

## Controlled comparison
Run the same timeline and evidence packet through three fictional two-pizza teams:
1. **Team Atlas (advisory):** the principles encourage reconsideration, but managers retain decision authority unless baseline protections intervene.
2. **Team Harbor (binding):** an independent review may substantiate a managerial-code violation and trigger corrective action.
3. **Team Lantern (constitutional):** employee may invoke enumerated rights and seek a binding interim remedy, subject to review and appeal.

## Documents per branch
- Engineer's incident report
- Service owner's release justification
- Security's technical finding
- HR/open-door intake record
- Outside-manager conflict-of-interest disclosure and determination
- Decision and appeal record
- Post-incident retrospective and amended precedent

## Evaluation
Measure in-universe time to response, deployment outcome, employee safety, process integrity, reviewer independence, cross-team effects, and trust. Keep facts consistent across branches. A legally required or nonwaivable baseline protection applies regardless of model.

## Narrative challenge
Write a sympathetic argument for each team's model, including the model whose outcome the author personally dislikes. Do not use a contrived villain to decide the experiment.
