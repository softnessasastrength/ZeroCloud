# CASE-LP-001: The cost of dissent

**Entirely fictional, no actual employer or employee depicted.**

## Scenario
The CTO authorizes a rushed deployment to meet a public demonstration deadline. A junior engineer identifies an untested authorization boundary and requests a delay. The CTO believes the risk is manageable; Finance has committed resources; the CISO wants a test; the employee invokes the open-door channel.

## Principles in tension
- Act decisively, document responsibly
- Dissent remains protected
- Authority carries accountability
- Results include how they were achieved

## Fictional artifacts to write
1. CTO deployment authorization: concise, strategic, impatient.
2. Engineer's dissent memorandum: technically specific, careful, concerned.
3. CISO risk assessment: adversarial toward assumptions, precise about unknowns.
4. CFO budget note: cost-conscious but not villainous.
5. Independent peer-manager review: procedural and evidence-led.
6. Board minutes: guarded institutional voice.
7. Retrospective and precedent: admits uncertainty and records what changes.

## Tests of the institution
- Is dissent protected when the CTO is right about urgency?
- Can a review pause a deployment or only recommend?
- What if the security concern turns out to be false?
- What if the employee was right but broke an access rule?
- Does the same standard apply to executives?

**No canonical outcome has been chosen.**
