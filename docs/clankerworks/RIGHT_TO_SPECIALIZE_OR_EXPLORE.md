# ClankerWorks: The Right to Specialize, Explore, or Stay

Status: proposed employee-development policy, documentation only. Additive to draft PR #13 and existing unmerged HR/corporate proposals.

## Principle
Curiosity is a right, not an obligation. Employees spending substantial time with machines must have opportunities to understand and improve them, but no employee must become an engineer, trainer, manager or generalist to be respected.

## Equally legitimate paths
- **Depth:** sustained excellence in a chosen process, including technical mastery and mentorship only if desired.
- **Breadth:** voluntary cross-training and lateral mobility.
- **Innovation:** optional process, tooling, documentation or software improvements with review and credit.
- **Stability:** continued good work in an existing role without an expectation of advancement or extracurricular learning.

These paths are nonhierarchical. Compensation, recognition, scheduling fairness and job security must not be conditioned on unpaid study, after-hours code contributions, or perpetual promotion-seeking. Depth specialists must have meaningful pay progression where feasible.

## ClankerClock and ClankerLearn behavior
Record preferences independently of competence and operational authorization. A worker may opt into specific learning topics, decline optional training, or select 'no additional development offers' without penalty. Required safety and role training remain distinct. Learning suggestions are nonintrusive and reversible. No inference that declining training means low engagement.

## Evaluation and fairness
Production evaluation measures assigned work with appropriate safety, quality and context adjustments. Optional learning hours, pull requests, mentoring and department switching are never used as hidden requirements for basic satisfactory performance. Audit assignment, pay and promotion decisions for bias toward highly visible volunteers. Support employees who change their minds over time.

## Synthetic acceptance scenarios
1. Excellent packer declines optional programming courses and remains eligible for normal compensation and recognition.
2. Curious picker requests robotics education and receives access to safe learning resources without production-floor authorization.
3. Specialist and generalist both receive fair performance evaluation under relevant task standards.
4. Employee changes from exploration to stability preference without losing existing qualifications.
5. No dashboard labels an employee 'unmotivated' for declining voluntary development.

## Open governance question
Define how to fund and calibrate specialist pay progression and optional learning opportunity allocation while preserving employee choice and operational coverage.
